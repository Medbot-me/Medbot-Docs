# Medical History

The medical history quick start helps you add your long-term conditions, medicines, allergies and old documents to your health record. You can skip any step and come back later.

Everything you add is labelled as reported by you until a clinician confirms it.

## Where to start

- On Home, the **Add your medical history** card. It shows while your record has no conditions, medicines or allergies. Choose **Start**, or **Review and add** if items are waiting ("You have N items waiting for your review."), or **Not now**.
- Or open the menu and select **Medical history** under **My health record**, or go to **Profile**, then **Preferences**, then **Medical history** (**Add or update**). That opens [your medical history](#your-medical-history).

The first screen lists four steps: **Conditions**, **Medicines and allergies**, **Documents** and **Review**. Select **Start adding history**, or **Skip for now**. Use **Save and exit** on any step to stop.

If you would rather have someone do it, see [Record Services](/patient/record-services).

## Your medical history

**Medical history** lists your conditions and your past surgeries and hospital stays, in one place. It says "Diagnoses from your clinic and the history you added. Update it whenever something changes."

Conditions are under **Long-term conditions**, **Current illness or injury** and **Past diagnoses**, then **Surgery and hospital stays**. Each row says when it started, how it is now, and who it came from: **From your clinic**, **From your document**, **Reported by you**, **Reported by a caregiver** or **Added by the Medbot team**. A condition a clinic's doctor added says "Added by" their name, the clinic and the date.

From here you can:

- Select **Add a condition**. See [Add a condition](#add-a-condition).
- Select **Upload documents**. See [Step 3: Documents](#step-3-documents).
- Select **Check them** when "N items are waiting for you to check", to review what Medbot found in your documents.
- Select **Mark as better now** on a condition you entered. It moves to **Past diagnoses** and is never deleted.
- [Change or remove](#change-or-remove-what-you-entered) a condition, medicine, surgery or hospital stay you entered.

The same conditions and medicines are in **Results**, under **More from your record**.

## Add a condition

Select **Add a condition**.

1. Under **What it is**, start typing to find it: "Pick a specific diagnosis, a broader one if you are not sure which kind, or keep your own words." Results show plain names, with the official code in small type. A broader choice is marked "any kind", for example "Type 2 diabetes, any kind", under "Not sure which kind? Choose a broader one". The last row, **Use my words**, saves exactly what you typed. When you choose a result, the page says "Matched to" the official name and its code.
2. Under **What kind of condition it is**, choose **Long-term**, **Short-term illness or injury** or **Not sure** ("Add it anyway. A clinician can sort it later.").
3. Say **When it started**: a **Month** and **Year**, or a year on its own.
4. Under **How it is now**, choose **Still have it** or **Better now**, and when it got better.
5. Optionally, under **Anything else your doctor should know**, add a note, for example when it happens or what you have tried.
6. Select **Add to my history**. The page says where it will be listed, "shown as reported by you until a clinician confirms it".

When it is added, you can attach photos or documents your doctor may find useful, such as a photo of a rash or a letter: up to 5 files, 10 MB each, JPEG, PNG, HEIC or PDF. You can also add or remove files later from the condition's own screen.

## Step 1: Conditions

1. Under "Any long-term conditions?", search, or pick from common ones such as High blood pressure, Type 2 diabetes or Asthma.
2. Add the year diagnosed (or Not sure), and mark it **Ongoing** or **Resolved**.
3. Select **Add a past surgery or hospital stay** if needed.
4. If none apply, select "I have no long-term conditions".
5. Select **Next: medicines**.

Conditions you add here are listed under **Long-term conditions**.

## Step 2: Medicines and allergies

1. Under "Medicines you take", include supplements and traditional medicine.
2. Photograph the labels, one photo per box or bottle. Names are kept exactly as printed and show **From label**. Or select **Type a medicine**. See [Typing a medicine](#typing-a-medicine).
3. Under allergies, answer "Are you allergic to any medicine, food or anything else?" with Yes, No or Not sure. For a yes, say what you are allergic to and what happened. Select **Add another allergy** for more.
4. Select **Next: documents**.

### Typing a medicine

As you type in **Medicine name**, Medbot suggests matching products registered in Singapore (from the Health Sciences Authority's list). Use the arrow keys or tap to choose one. A suggestion with a similar spelling, rather than the name you typed, says "Similar spelling. Check this is your medicine." Medbot never puts a look-alike name in for you.

"Not on the list? Just keep typing and we will save what you typed."

Then fill in what you know:

- **Strength (optional)**. A product you chose from the list fills this in.
- **Dose and how often (optional)**.
- **Route (how you use it)**, for example **By mouth (oral)**, **Breathed in (inhaler)** or **Skin patch**. A product from the list suggests its usual route. Choose **Not sure** if you do not know.

If you change the name after choosing a product, the strength and route that came with it are cleared.

## Step 3: Documents

Select **Take a photo** or **Choose files**. Use PDF, JPG or PNG files. Medbot reads each one and shows how many items it found. If a file cannot be read, it tells you why, for example because it is too blurry or not a medical document.

For a document of several pages, choose **Several pages** to send the photos as one document. If a photo looks blurry or cut off, Medbot suggests taking it again.

When Medbot finds lab values in a document, it shows **Check N values**. Select it to compare them with the paper and save them. See [Scanned Lab Reports](/patient/lab-reports).

Nothing is added until you approve it on the next screen. The original files are kept under **Documents** in your record. Select **Next: review**.

## Step 4: Review

On **Check before it is added**, untick anything that is wrong. If two documents show different doses, choose which you take now, or pick "Neither, or I'm not sure". Each item shows where it came from: you entered it, your label photo, or an uploaded document.

Select "Add N items to my record". Not sure what is right? Select "Ask the Medbot team to reconcile it" (a paid service, see [Record Services](/patient/record-services)).

When done, you see "Added to your record" with links to your conditions, medicines and allergies. They also appear under **More from your record** on the [Results](/patient/results#more-from-your-record) tab.

## Change or remove what you entered

Under a condition, medicine, surgery or hospital stay that you entered, select **Edit** or **Remove**. A caregiver with "Can make changes" access can do the same for the person they act for. The same buttons are under a medicine or condition in **Results**, under **More from your record**.

- **Edit** opens it in a form: "Change what you entered, then save. It stays marked as reported by you, not confirmed by a clinic. Your record keeps each earlier version and who changed it." For a medicine you can say whether you are **Still taking** it or have **Stopped**. If it was changed somewhere else in the meantime, the page says so and asks you to open it again.
- **Remove** asks you to confirm with **Remove** again, or **Keep**: "Remove &lt;name&gt;? It stays in your record, marked as entered by mistake."

You cannot change or remove what a clinic recorded. It was written by the clinic. If something there is wrong, message the clinic.

## A clinic's review

If you turn on **Share my medical history** for a clinic under [Sharing with your clinics](/patient/clinic-sharing#share-my-medical-history), its doctors and nurses can see the conditions you entered. A doctor can code a condition, which gives it an official name and code, and confirm it, amend its wording or details, remove one that is a duplicate or not a medical condition, or add one. Every change is kept with who made it, and your own words are kept.

When a doctor has reviewed something, you get a notification, **Your medical history was reviewed**. Your list shows:

- A **Reviewed** badge, and a banner: "&lt;clinic&gt; reviewed the entries marked Reviewed. Check they look right, and edit anything that does not."
- "Confirmed by" or "Coded by" the doctor, the clinic and the date, and "Official name:" with the name and code. If the doctor reworded it, "Also reported as:" shows your own words.
- Any files you attached, and your own note.

If you change what a condition is, the doctor's review is removed and it goes back to unconfirmed: "Changing what the condition is removes the review by" the doctor. Changing only the dates, kind or how it is now keeps the review.

Under **Removed by your clinic**, you see what a clinic took out of your history, with who removed it, when and why ("duplicate of", "not a medical condition" or "entered by mistake") and any note. Select **Restore** if it belongs there. It comes back as unconfirmed.

Caregivers do not see the quick start while acting for someone else.
