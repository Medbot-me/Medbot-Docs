# Dosing Rules

A dosing rule holds the limits for dosing one oral liquid medicine by the patient's weight, for example paracetamol syrup. When a rule is published and names a formulary product, the [prescribing form](/cms/prescribing#oral-liquids-dosed-by-weight) works the dose out from the patient's weight and holds the order to the rule. A medicine with no published rule is prescribed as usual. Rules belong to the clinic. Find them at **Pharmacy → Dosing rules** (`/dosing-rules`).

## Who can do this

| Task | Clinic Admin | Receptionist | Doctor | Nurse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Read the rules and their limits | Yes | No | Yes | No |
| Create, edit, publish, retire or reinstate a rule | Yes | No | No | No |
| Keep your own version of a rule's limits | No | No | Yes | No |
| Read the **Dose overrides** log | Yes | No | No | No |

A Receptionist or Nurse who opens `/dosing-rules` sees "This action requires one of the following roles: admin, doctor." The page is for the people who set the limits and the doctors who answer for the orders. A nurse who drafts a weight-based dose is held to the clinic's published rule in the prescribing form.

## The list

The page lists one row for each medicine, with **Status**, the **Active** version, an open **Draft**, the formulary **Products** the rule doses and when it was **Updated**. A doctor also sees a **Your version** column.

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Active** | A version is published, so prescribing uses it. |
| **Not published** | The rule has only a draft. Prescribing does not use it. |
| **Retired** | New orders are no longer dosed by it. |

Until a rule is published, every medicine is prescribed with a free-text dose.

## Creating a rule (Clinic Admin)

1. Select **New dosing rule**. Start from a blank rule, or from the paracetamol template with **Start from the paracetamol template**. The template holds the recommended oral paracetamol limits. Review them before you publish.
2. Enter the **Medicine**, for example "Ibuprofen". The route is always oral.
3. Fill in the limits, in four cards:

   | Card | Fields |
   | --- | --- |
   | **Dose per kg** | **Minimum per dose** and **Maximum per dose** (mg/kg), an optional **Maximum single dose** (mg), and the **Measuring step** (mL) a dose is measured in |
   | **Frequency** | **Shortest interval** and **Longest interval** (hours), and **Most doses in 24 h** |
   | **Daily limits** | **Maximum per day** (mg/kg), **Absolute maximum per day** (mg), **Severe limit policy**, and **Weight must be newer than** (months) |
   | **Who it applies to** | **Age from (over)** and an optional **Age to**, each in months or years |

   **Severe limit policy** decides what happens when a dose goes past a hard limit such as the single-dose or daily maximum: **Reason only** (the prescriber gives a reason), **Reason + co-sign** (a reason and a second clinician) or **Block the order**.
4. Under **Products**, search the formulary with **Add a product** and choose each oral liquid the rule doses. Medbot reads the concentration from the product's name, for example 120 mg/5 mL, and you can correct it. A rule doses only the products it lists.
5. Optionally add a **Change note (optional)**, for example "Lower maximum per dose after the pharmacy review".
6. Select **Save draft**, or **Publish v1**.

The editor lists what still needs fixing before you can publish, for example "1 problem to fix before publishing", and shows **To confirm** notes for combinations that look unintended, such as an interval that gives more doses a day than the rule allows.

## Versions

A published version never changes. To change a rule, select **Edit as draft v&lt;n&gt;**, change the limits, and **Publish v&lt;n&gt;**. The editor shows the **Differences from v&lt;n&gt;** as you go. **Discard draft** drops it. The **Versions** card lists every version with what changed.

Orders keep the version they were checked against: the rule, its version, the weight used and the range are recorded on each order.

**Retire** stops new orders being dosed by the rule, and keeps its versions for the orders that used them. **Reinstate** brings a retired rule back. A retired rule cannot be published until it is reinstated.

## Your own version (Doctor)

A doctor answers for the orders they write, so the clinic's limits are a starting point and not a ceiling. Open a rule to read the clinic's limits, and keep your own version of them.

1. Open the rule from the list. The page shows **Clinic rule v&lt;n&gt;** and **Your version**.
2. Change the limits you want different, in the same cards. **Differences from the clinic rule** lists what you changed.
3. Select **Save my version**.

Your orders then start on your version, and you can switch to the clinic rule for one order in the prescribing form. **Use the clinic rule** drops your version and goes back to it. Every save and every return to the clinic rule is kept under **Your record for this rule**.

When the clinic publishes a newer version of a rule you adapted, your version **needs review**. Until you review it, your orders start on the clinic's rule, and you can still choose your version for an order. The rule appears as a task on **Tasks** with **Review**. On the rule's page, **What the clinic changed** shows the difference. A limit you both changed takes the clinic's value for you to adjust. Select **Save my version on clinic v&lt;n&gt;** to keep your version on the new clinic version, or **Use the clinic rule**.

## The dose overrides log

**Pharmacy → Dose overrides** (`/reports/dose-overrides`, Clinic Admins only) is meant to list every time a prescriber went past a dosing rule: the patient and weight, the dose prescribed, the range it was checked against, which limit it passed, the reason, the prescriber and the rule version. Entries cannot be edited or deleted. Overrides given while prescribing are not saved to this log yet, so for now it reads "No dose overrides recorded yet." The reasons are on each order.
