# Roles & Permissions

> **Note:** Roles are set per organisation. A person can be a Clinic Admin at one clinic and a Nurse at another, or hold more than one role in the same clinic, such as Doctor and Clinic Admin. They work as one role at a time, chosen from the [sidebar switcher or the **Working as** menu](/cms/administration/switching#switching-role), and every check below runs against that active role in the organisation they have currently chosen. Roles do not add up: to do a Clinic Admin task, switch to Clinic Admin. A Clinic Admin [previewing another role](/cms/administration/switching#previewing-another-role) sees that role's navigation but keeps the Clinic Admin column below.

Medbot CMS is built around four main clinic roles, two further roles for counselling and allied health staff, and one internal account that is not a real clinic role. Every action re-checks the signed-in user's role before it reads or writes clinic data. What you see on a page only controls what is visible, so a role's real boundary is enforced at the action, not on the screen. When a role cannot do something, the control is hidden or disabled, and an action attempted anyway is refused.

This page is the master list. Every **Who can do this** table on a feature page agrees with the matrix below. The matrix has a column for each of the four main roles. The two further roles are set out in [Patient service associate and Allied health professional](#patient-service-associate-and-allied-health-professional) and are not repeated in each row.

## The four clinic roles

**Clinic Admin** is the clinic's operational lead. A Clinic Admin manages staff, registers patients, books and edits appointments, manages locations and session setup, operates the reception queue, records and corrects vitals, records vaccinations, manages the formulary and catalog, composes documents, files and manages diagnostic results, manages the results review rota, publishes the roster, and manages attendance. In short, a Clinic Admin can act as a backstop for most workflows another role primarily owns.

**Receptionist** runs the front desk. A Receptionist registers patients, updates contact details and communication consent, books and edits appointments, manages today's session setup, operates the reception queue, files and views diagnostic results, and views and prints existing patient documents. A Receptionist can build and adjust roster drafts, but only a Clinic Admin can publish a week.

**Doctor** and **Nurse** read patient, practitioner, and appointment data through their clinical workflows. Both record and correct vitals, record vaccinations, view the formulary, and view and print patient documents. Both compose letters and memos, file and view diagnostic results, and draft result explanations for patients. A Doctor can additionally compose referrals, issue medical certificates, sign off, reassign, or withdraw results, and approve, send, or withdraw result explanations. A Nurse cannot. Neither role registers patients, books appointments, nor manages staff, sessions, the queue, the formulary, the results rota, or roster publishing.

## Patient service associate and Allied health professional

Two further roles can be given to staff from the [Staff](/cms/administration/staff) page. Neither prescribes, orders, dispenses, manages inventory or submits claims.

**Patient service associate** counsels patients, for example on their costs, and works the front desk's queue. A Patient service associate:

- works the [reception queue](/cms/front-desk/reception-queue) as a Receptionist does: checks patients in, adds walk-ins, calls patients, routes them, takes payments, and cancels and reopens appointments. They cannot open [Session Setup](/cms/front-desk/session-setup) or mark a visit **Completed**
- reads accounts and invoices on [Billing](/cms/billing), but does not create or change them, and does not see the catalog
- opens and reads patient charts, starts and closes encounters, and writes and claims progress notes
- leads **Financial counselling** consults
- sees results, and reviews patients' requests to change their name or date of birth
- does not register patients. That stays with Receptionists and Clinic Admins.

**Allied health professional** is a dietitian, physiotherapist, pharmacist or health educator. The profession is chosen when the role is given, and shown beside it, for example "Allied health professional · Dietitian". An Allied health professional:

- sees the whole location's queue, read-only, and can leave remarks on it
- opens and reads patient charts, starts and closes encounters, and writes and claims progress notes
- leads **Health education** consults, and the consult their profession names: **Diet counselling** for a dietitian, **Physiotherapy** for a physiotherapist and **Pharmacy counselling** for a pharmacist
- sees results.

A Doctor or Nurse can lead every consult kind. Both further roles can see the roster, and both open on a service's **Workstation**. They cannot write a consultation note, nurse note, referral, certificate, order or prescription, or dispense.

## The Superuser account

Superuser is not a real clinic role. It is an internal and testing account that passes every role check, so one login can exercise every role's work without swapping accounts. It is not offered on the staff form; it is set directly on an account. Because it bypasses the role list rather than being granted the sum of every role's permissions, a few controls that look for one specific role stay hidden to it. Signing off a result, composing on a patient's Documents, issuing a medical certificate, and the Displays panel on Session setup all require signing in as the role that owns them. Approving a result explanation also needs a linked clinician profile, which a Superuser account usually lacks.

> **Note:** Platform administration screens are seen only by Medbot platform superadmins, a Medbot staff identity that is not a clinic role. No clinic role, including Clinic Admin, gives access. See [Platform Administration](/cms/administration/platform-administration).

A medical certificate prints the issuing doctor's MCR number, so only the Doctor role issues one. A Clinic Admin who is also a doctor switches to their Doctor role to issue it. A Clinic Admin can still void one.

Concerns, shared chats and answer reports hold the patient's own words, so only doctors and nurses read them, and a Superuser account is refused too.

A Doctor sees only the patients assigned to them in the queue; a Nurse sees every patient at the location. Reading a chart, its vitals, and its notes is open to all four roles for a patient in the organisation.
