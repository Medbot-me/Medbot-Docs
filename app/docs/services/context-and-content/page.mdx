# Service Context & Content

Two things tell a service's AI what it needs to know: **SOP and Instructions**, a plain-language description of how the service runs, and the **content library**, the documents agents and AI encounters can draw on.

## SOP and Instructions

The **SOP and Instructions** card on the service's **Overview** (and step 2 of the setup wizard) holds free text that Medbot turns into the **agent context** given to every service agent run. Describe:

- how the service runs and the steps patients and staff go through
- who does what, and when
- when to escalate, and to whom
- how to use the content library, e.g. "Answer dietary questions from the 'Post-op nutrition' handout in the content library"

A starting set of protocols is filled in for a new service. To change it:

1. On **Overview**, click **Edit protocols and instructions**.
2. Edit the text.
3. Click **Save**. The button reads "Saving and generating agent context…" while Medbot rebuilds the agent context, and a "Protocols saved" message confirms it.

The generated agent context sits collapsed under the text, so you can check what agents will actually be given. There is no graph or builder; it is just text. It replaces the old program **Workflow** text.

### Other context agents receive

The SOP is one layer. Agents also receive:

- each **care team member's** agent context, built from their preferences (see [Care Team](/docs/services/care-team#members))
- each **patient's** context, built from their encounter notes (see [Patients & Cohort](/docs/services/patients#encounter-notes))

## Content library

The **Content library** item in the sidebar (and the wizard's **Content library** step) holds the clinical resources this service's agents and AI encounters can use: documents, and pictures that agents can show patients.

### Adding a document

- **Upload a document or picture**: choose a PDF, Word, Excel or CSV file, give it a **Title** and an optional **Description**, and pick its type:
  - **Reference material**: background the assistant can quote from, such as a handout or guideline.
  - **Counselling protocol**: a structured script for a counselling or education session. A spreadsheet is split into tables of rows, not session steps, so it usually belongs under reference material.

  Click **Upload & add to library**.
- **Attach an existing resource**: pick a document you have already uploaded elsewhere in Medbot.

A new document is processed before it can be used; until then it shows as pending or processing. **Remove** takes a document out of this service's library, and **Mark as reference** or **Mark as protocol** changes its type.

### Adding a picture

A picture is reference material an agent can **show the patient** while it explains: a diagram, a device, a breakdown of costs. Use the same **Upload a document or picture** form:

1. Choose a JPEG, PNG or WebP file. A preview appears, and the type selector disappears: a picture is always reference material.
2. Give it a **Title** and a **Caption**: "what the picture shows (patients see this)". The caption is required. It is what the agent reads to decide whether to show the picture, and what the patient reads under it.
3. Click **Upload & add to library**. Camera details such as location are removed on upload.

In the **Reference material** list the picture shows as a thumbnail with its title, caption and a picture badge. It cannot be marked as a protocol.

### Where the library is used

- **AI encounters** can have documents attached by default, attach the best matches automatically, and let the assistant search the library. See [AI Encounters](/docs/services/encounters#settings).
- **Service agents** with the **Clinical resources** tool can look things up in it.
- The **patient agent** answers health-education questions from it, and walks patients through counselling protocols with its **Education & counselling sessions** tool.
- **Session agents** on the patient portal (history taking, health education and financial counselling) can show a picture from the library under their reply, with its title and caption. Patients tap it to open it full size, on the web and in the mobile app.
